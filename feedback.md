# Feedback: real people vs. AI

Book reviewed: *Jack and the Beanstalk, starring Reuvi* (5-page PDF: cover, pages 1–3, and an
About Me page with the real photo next to the cartoon). Every reviewer, human or AI, saw the
same PDF and answered the same four questions:

1. **Likeness**: does the cartoon look like the person in the real photo?
   (Yes, it clearly looks like them / Somewhat / A little bit / Not at all)
2. **Story**: does the story ring true to Jack and the Beanstalk?
   (Yes, completely / Mostly / Not really / Not at all)
3. **Professional**: does it look like a book you could buy?
   (Yes, very professional / Mostly / Not really / Not at all)
4. **What isn't good?**

---

## Part 1: 10 real responses

### How they were collected

The survey was built in CloudResearch Engage. Engage's free plan doesn't include a public
anonymous link, so I sent the survey to friends and family through Engage's preview link, and
they viewed the PDF on Google Drive. 10 people finished the whole survey; partial and test
sessions are left out. All 10 passed the attention check ("What animal does Jack sell?" →
"A cow"). The responses are anonymous, and R1–R10 are in the order Engage listed them.

**Caveat:** everyone here knows me, and most are family. That makes "does it look like him"
easier to judge than it would be for a stranger, and probably makes the answers kinder overall.

| # | Likeness | Story | Professional | What isn't good? |

| R1 | Yes, clearly | Mostly | Yes, very | "It didn't have an ending!!! Where is the beanstalk?" |

| R3 | Yes, clearly | Yes, completely | Mostly | "I didn't like the text font and color, that could look better." |
| R4 | Somewhat | Not really | Not at all | "It said he traded 5 beans, but the picture has 6 beans." |

## Part 2: 10 AI responses

### How they were collected

I used three different AI models, in three separate chats. I attached the same PDF in each and
gave each model its own set of personas to play:

| Model | Personas |
|---|---|
| Claude Sonnet (medium effort) | S1 grandmother ("Bubby"), S2 grandfather ("Zayde"), S3 rabbi |
| Claude Haiku (medium effort) | H1 10-year-old boy, H2 15-year-old girl, H3 friend since preschool |
| Claude Opus 4.7 (low effort) | O1 mother, O2 math professor, O3 friend from class, O4 middle-aged man who just met me |

Each model first gave free-form feedback, and then I asked it to answer the same four survey
questions. The structured answers are below. Haiku also added its own answers as itself; those
aren't counted, since they don't come from one of the personas.

### Responses

| # | Persona (model) | Likeness | Story | Professional | What isn't good? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Grandmother (Sonnet) | Yes, clearly | Mostly | Mostly | The cow in the bean scene looks "too posed and glamorous for a farm animal." |
| S2 | Grandfather (Sonnet) | Yes, clearly | Mostly | Yes, very | The pacing is rushed: it goes from the cover to "beans out the window" fast, with no beanstalk yet. |
| S3 | Rabbi (Sonnet) | Yes, clearly | Mostly | Mostly | Nothing wrong with the craft. The content note is that the tale's moral (stealing works out if you're poor) is worth talking through with young readers. |
| H1 | 10-year-old boy (Haiku) | Yes, clearly | Yes, completely | Mostly | The beans look like jellybeans, so it isn't clear they're magic. The giant should be in it, because that's the best part. |
| H2 | 15-year-old girl (Haiku) | Somewhat | Mostly | Mostly | The bottom third of the About Me page is empty, and its fonts don't match the cover. |
| H3 | Friend since preschool (Haiku) | Somewhat | Mostly | Not really | "The hat is a little much," and with no giant or fight scene it doesn't feel like Jack and the Beanstalk. |

### AI ratings at a glance

| Question | Yes / best | Middle (Somewhat, Mostly) | Low (A little bit, Not really) | Not at all |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Likeness | 6 | 4 | 0 | 0 |
| Story | 1 | 7 | 2 | 0 |
| Professional | 2 | 7 | 1 | 0 |

### How the three models differed

- **Sonnet** was the warmest and stayed in character the most, with Yiddish phrases and family
  warmth. It was the least critical: every persona said the cartoon clearly looks like me, and
  none rated anything below "Mostly." Its most original point was the rabbi's comment about the
  story's moral, which came from the persona's values rather than from the pictures.
- **Haiku** gave the most concrete visual details, such as the beans looking like jellybeans
  and the empty space on the About Me page. It also made the most mistakes about the PDF. In
  its free-form answer it described a cow on the cover and a fourth "planting" page, and the
  book has neither.
- **Opus** gave the most critical story ratings: both of the AI's "Not really" ratings for
  story came from Opus. However, its four personas mostly made the same two complaints (the
  story ends too soon, and age 23 doesn't fit a children's book). The personas changed the tone
  more than the content.

## Part 3: Comparison

### Ratings side by side

| Question | Real: best / middle / low / worst | AI: best / middle / low / worst |
|---|---|---|
| Likeness | 6 / 3 / 0 / 1 | 6 / 4 / 0 / 0 |
| Story | 1 / 5 / 4 / 0 | 1 / 7 / 2 / 0 |
| Professional | 4 / 2 / 2 / 2 | 2 / 7 / 1 / 0 |

- **Likeness:** almost identical. Both groups had 6 "Yes, clearly." The AI could compare the
  photo to the cartoon about as well as people who actually know me.
- **Story:** real people were harsher, with 4 "Not really" against the AI's 2. The AI mostly
  settled on "Mostly."
- **Professional:** this is the biggest difference. Real people split sharply (4 "very," 2 "not
  at all"). The AI said "Mostly" 7 times and never chose "Not at all."
- **Overall:** the AI never picked the worst answer on any question. Real people did 3 times.

### What both groups caught

| Problem | Real | AI |
|---|---|---|
| The book ends too soon, with no beanstalk or giant | R1, R2, R10 | S2, H1, H3, O1, O2, O3, O4 |
| It seems young for a 23-year-old | R8, R9 | O1, O2, O3, O4 |
| The font and text styling could look better | R3 | H2, O2 |
| Something is off with the beans | R4 (6 beans, not 5) | H1 (they look like candy) |

The missing ending was the clearest signal. 7 of the 10 AI reviewers and 3 of the 10 real
reviewers raised it on their own.

### What only the AI caught

- **The About Me page:** its placement (better on the back cover), the empty space at the
  bottom, and fonts that don't match the rest of the book.
- **The cow:** it looks posed rather than like a farm animal.
- **The story's moral:** a values point about the story itself, not the art.
- **Positives:** the kippah stays on Jack throughout.

### What only real people caught or gave

- **The exact bean count:** a real person counted 6 beans where the text says 5. No AI model
  noticed this, even though the models looked at the same page.
- **Content, not just layout:** R8 questioned what the About Me favorites say about me. The AI
  commented only on where the page is and how it looks.
- **Strong opinions both ways:** "Wouldn't change a thing," "Brilliant craftsmanship," and also
  "It's literally not a book." The AI stayed in the middle.
- **Off-topic answers:** car chases, and a request to star a sibling instead. The AI "friend"
  personas copied the joking tone (O3's "you're lying" is close to R9's "are you in college or
  wut"), but they always got back to useful feedback.

### Where the AI got it wrong

- Haiku described things that aren't in the book: a cow on the cover and a planting page.
- Sonnet's grandfather praised the "panel-to-panel consistency," but the cow changes between
  pages 1 and 2.
- Real people make mistakes too, but none of them described pages that don't exist.

### Takeaways

1. **Persona and model change the tone more than the verdict.** The grandmother sounds nothing
   like the friend from class, but 7 of the 10 AI reviewers made the same main complaint. The
   three models differed more from each other than personas within one model did.
2. **The AI is good at finding general problems** (no ending, the About Me page, the age), but
   it missed the most concrete flaw, the bean count. It also invented details that aren't there.
3. **The AI is a poor stand-in for real ratings.** It matched people on likeness, but on
   professionalism it bunched in the middle where real people split. It never gave a strongly
   negative answer, so an AI-only panel would make the book look safer than it is.
4. **When both groups flag the same thing, it's the top priority:** finish the story (the
   beanstalk and the giant), fix the beans, and make the text and the About Me page look like
   part of a real book.
